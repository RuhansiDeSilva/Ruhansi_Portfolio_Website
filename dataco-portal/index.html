At-a-Glance: How the Project Was Built in 6 Key Steps

From 180,519 raw e-commerce rows to a focused 575-order urgent escalation list.

Step 1 • Data & EDA

180,519 Order Items

Analyzed 65,752 unique orders (~2.75 items/order) across 5 global markets. Found 54.83% of all items arrive late and Shipping Mode strongly drives delay risk.

Step 2 • Leakage Audit

Strict Checkout Cutoff

Removed post-order leakage (real shipping days, delivery status, canceled/fraud order status), PII (names, emails, passwords), and exact financial duplicates.

Step 3 • Group Split & Top 100

Zero Order Overlap

Split strictly by Order ID (85% Train / 7.5% Val / 7.5% Test). Selected the Top 100 encoded features via 5-Fold Group CV, cutting 83.6% of noise (+0.0366 AUC).

Step 4 • Lens 1 Ensemble

5 Tuned Models Vote

Combined tuned Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, LightGBM, and Calibrated Linear SVM (20% weight each) at a balanced 0.50 threshold.

Step 5 • Lens 2 Order Triage

Risk × Dollar Value

Averaged item probabilities per unique order (4,932 test orders) and paired Low/Med/High Risk bands with training dollar quantiles ($314.55 & $625.71).

Step 6 • Business Impact

Focused Escalation

Achieved 72.00% order accuracy and isolated 575 Urgent orders (11.66% of workload) with an 83.83% actual late rate covering 20.63% ($502.7K) of order value.

1 Project Overview & Dual-Lens Purpose

This project developed an integrated machine learning and business decision-support system for predicting late deliveries and prioritizing shipments within the DataCo Supply Chain dataset.

Rather than stopping at raw item-level predictions, the architecture bridges two operational lenses:

  • Lens 1 (Prediction):5-model soft-voting ensemble predicting late-delivery probability at order placement.
  • Lens 2 (Prioritization):Aggregates predictions to unique customer orders and combines risk with dollar exposure.
  • Prediction Point:Order placement time (strictly zero post-order leakage).

2 Final System Configuration (Experiment 2)

  • Final Model ArchitectureFive-Model Soft Voting Ensemble
  • Base Classifiers (20% weight each)LR, DT, RF, LightGBM, Calibrated SVM
  • Selected Feature SetTop 100 Encoded Features (from 611)
  • Classification Operating Threshold0.50 (Balances Precision & Accuracy)
  • Lens 2 Risk BoundariesLow < 0.30 | Med 0.30–0.65 | High ≥ 0.65
  • Monetary Impact Quantiles (Train)Low < $314.55 | High ≥ $625.71

7 Feature Importance & Error Analysis Discoveries

Across all five tuned models, Shipping Mode and its interactions with region and product category were the dominant predictors.

  • Standard Class Bottleneck:100% of validation False Negatives (3,033 missed late items) occurred in Standard Class shipments.
  • First Class & Same Day Precision:First Class achieved 94.29% accuracy (0 FN) and Same Day achieved 97.74% accuracy (0 FN, 17 FP).
  • Geographic Stability:Error rates varied only modestly across global markets, with LATAM showing slightly higher error (29.79%) than Africa (24.67%).

8 Key Project Limitations & Next Steps

  • Recall Ceiling (~59%):At threshold 0.50, ~41% of late deliveries (primarily Standard Class) remain undetected.
  • Shipping Mode Dependence:Models rely heavily on Shipping Mode SLA differences and struggle to separate late vs on-time items inside Standard Class.
  • Order-Level Calibration:Order risk is the arithmetic mean of line probabilities rather than an independently calibrated order-level model.
  • Rule-Based Triage Costs:Low/Medium/Urgent cutoffs use domain rules rather than empirical intervention cost-benefit curves.
🏁 9. Final Project Conclusion & Deployment Roadmap

The DataCo Supply Chain Late-Delivery Prediction project provides a validated proof of concept connecting machine learning classification (Lens 1: 71.69% line accuracy, 84.81% precision) with business-impact shipment prioritization (Lens 2: 72.00% order accuracy, 85.28% high-risk precision, and 83.83% late rate in the 575-order Urgent tier). Before production deployment, future work should train specialized sub-models for Standard Class shipments to boost recall, calibrate aggregated order-level probabilities, and incorporate real expediting costs into the priority matrix.
